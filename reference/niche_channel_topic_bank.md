> We use maps, data, and visual storytelling to explain how the world’s systems work, why they exist where they do, and what happens when they break.

3. The Geography of Broken Systems

# Episode 1

## Why Bangkok Keeps Flooding

**Subtitle:** *The Geography of a City Built Against Water*

### Suggested 8-Minute Structure

| Time | Section |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:35 | Cold open: reveal Bangkok’s elevation and flood exposure |
| 0:35–1:40 | Bangkok was built for water |
| 1:40–3:00 | Urbanisation changed the relationship with water |
| 3:00–4:30 | Land subsidence and why centimeters matter |
| 4:30–5:45 | Rain, river, tide and drainage pressure |
| 5:45–6:50 | Why bigger drains alone cannot solve the problem |
| 6:50–7:35 | Future risk and expansion |
| 7:35–8:00 | Final takeaway |

**Core thesis:**

> Bangkok’s flooding problem is not simply bad drainage. It is the result of building one of the world’s largest cities on a landscape that was designed by nature to flood.

# Topic Selection Framework

Before greenlighting a topic, ask:

1. **Can the story be understood better through a map?**
2. **Is there a surprising pattern, bottleneck, route, or constraint?**
3. **Can we access trustworthy data or public maps?**
4. **Does the subject have a strong visual reveal?**
5. **Can it sustain roughly 8 minutes without filler?**
6. **Is there a clear causal story rather than a list of facts?**
7. **Can the episode generate 2–5 Shorts afterward?**
